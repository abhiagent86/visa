01 · VISION

Best-in-class B2B sales operations

Two lenses on one engine. Operations: how the sales machine is designed, planned, governed and improved. Field: how every client is won and then grown through adoption, value and expansion. Four metrics connect them: AANR, time to revenue, realisation and net revenue retention. Common operating mechanics make it run the same way in every market.

A commercial control tower that maximises net revenue per incentive dollar and shortens the time from signature to revenue, run as one connected system from plan to realised revenue, with AI doing the routine work.

What changes when the journey runs past the first sale

New north-star metric

Net revenue retention

This year's net revenue from last year's clients, divided by last year's. It captures adoption, expansion and renewal in one number, the way software companies track their installed base.

New motion

Client success for VAS

A light client-success role, or pod, that owns adoption and time to first value for value-added services, working with client services and VCA.

Pay and credit

Reward consumption and expansion

Credit expansion AANR and realised usage, not only first bookings, so client teams stay invested after the signature.

Handover: the pre-close activation plan

Time to revenue starts before signature

What the plan contains

  • Migration or integration timeline and a committed go-live date
  • Client resources and the joint governance for the project
  • Client goals with baseline KPIs: active cards, spend per card, token use, VAS usage
  • Activation programme, including planned use of VIK
  • Known risks and who owns them
How it is governed

Who signs, and what is measured

  • Led by the client team; signed off by implementation for incentive deals above a threshold, before deal approval
  • After signature, the post-sales team leads and the client team supports
  • Measured by: clients with no confirmed path to go-live · time to go-live · share of deals with a signed-off plan

Consumption vs usage: a diagnostic for every key client

Consumption vs deal model ↑
High consumption, narrow useDependent on one product. Cross-sell gap: tokens, risk tools, data, commercial cards.
High consumption, broad useHealthy. Expand, explore an early renewal, use as a reference.
Low consumption, narrow useRenewal risk. Recovery plan, executive engagement, reason code logged.
Low consumption, broad useAdopted but volume behind plan. Realisation risk: activation campaigns, VIK-funded programmes.
Breadth of product use →

Usage above the contracted tier is an upsell or re-pricing signal; usage below it is logged as renewal risk with a mitigation plan.

Renewal risk and client engagement practices

Risk

AANR-at-risk register

Every at-risk client has AANR at risk, a standard reason code (competitor offer, domestic-scheme mandate, incentive gap, product gap, merger), notes and a retention plan. Product gaps go back to product teams.

Renewal

Owned early, priced deliberately

A named renewal owner 18–24 months before expiry; renewal forecast for the next four quarters; incentive cost of renewal (step-up per dollar of AANR retained) tracked by the deal desk.

Engagement

Value proven with the client

Standard client business reviews showing value against documented goals; an annual relationship assessment for Tier 1–2 clients; client success stories developed as references.

Why it matters

From "did we hit target?" to "what drove it?"

The bridge splits growth into market, sales (share, value-added services) and pricing effects, and shows what is leaking. Rolled up by cluster it turns business reviews into decisions.

Clients rarely leave a network outright and volume moves with the economy, so report net revenue retention both as reported and market-adjusted.

1D

Quota and incentives

Pay for what is real, defend and grow separately, and set quotas on potential.

Pay on what's real

Net, realised revenue

Keep AANR net of incentives. Pay part at signing and go-live and true up the rest to realised revenue at 12 months. Reduce payout when a deal exceeds the market's incentive-to-gross ceiling.

Defend and grow

Separate retention from growth

Incentives step up at renewal, so net revenue can fall even when a renewal is won. Measure renewals with a retention objective and an incentive ceiling; credit expansion AANR separately.

Fair quotas

Potential, not history

Blend the top-down plan gap with bottom-up account potential from transaction data. Aim for 60–70% of sellers at or above quota, with plans issued within 30–45 days of the year starting.

Pipeline health: four dimensions

Coverage

Is there enough?

Pipeline vs quota over a rolling four quarters; forecast pipeline vs the next two quarters' quota. Renewals planned separately from the contract calendar.

Quality

Is it real?

Share of pipeline aged over a year or untouched for 30 days; qualification score completeness.

Pace

Is it moving?

Average days per stage; stalled pipeline; stage-to-stage conversion.

Balance

Is it well spread?

Spread across stages, products and clients; how much is due to close at quarter-end versus earlier in the quarter.

Run as a monthly conversation between each sales manager and seller, with action plans, and rolled up twice a quarter in a regional demand-health checkpoint with marketing and partners.

Deal management phased by quarter

Two quarters out

Qualify

Is there a deal, should we invest, can we win? Fortnightly qualification board; deal scored on eight criteria.

Next quarter

Progress

Deal reviews: is it progressing, is the solution validated by the client, is there a mutual close plan? Deal model started with the deal desk.

This quarter

Close

Weekly top-deal reviews above a size threshold; slipped-deal review in the last month; approvals and contract on a fixed path.

Deal score: value storyDeal score: engagement plan
Compelling event · client challenges · business value drivers · solution differentiatorsStakeholder strategy · competitors (incl. domestic schemes, A2A) · partners and ecosystem · funding and incentive economics

Each criterion is rated weak, moderate or strong. Combined with IDDEA for how to sell, the score shows how likely a deal is to close and feeds the forecast.

Forecast gains and losses together

Net AANR forecast = new and expansion AANR − losses (renewal step-downs, leakage to domestic schemes and A2A, full losses), for this quarter and next, with a rolling two-year view of renewal risk.

Managers' judgement (best and worst case) is layered on a system forecast built from clean CRM data, after a hygiene check before each review. Over time, AI forecasts sit alongside both.

Forecast quality is measured too: predictability (the week-2 call vs the final result) · how much is due to close at quarter-end · next quarter's forecast ready by month 2.

How to build a use case: the deal-desk copilot

Value case → workflow → data → solution → backtest and pilot → scale. AI prepares, compares and flags; people decide.

  1. Value case

    Incentive deals are slow to model and approve; underperformers are found after signing. Levers: better incentive allocation, faster approvals, fewer misses.

  2. Workflow

    Pre-fill the deal model from transaction data, generate base and downside scenarios, benchmark against how similar deals performed, route the approval, draft the memo.

  3. Data

    Client transaction history, past deal models alongside their actual results, CRM context, contract templates and pricing standards.

  4. Solution & guardrails

    Built on the existing CRM and cloud stack. No automatic approvals; every number traceable; audit log; client data stays in-house.

  5. Backtest

    Run it on two to three years of past deals: would it have flagged the ones that underperformed?

  6. Pilot

    One cluster, 20–30 deals, against a comparison group. Targets: half the preparation time; approvals in one week instead of three.

  7. Stop or scale

    Stop if it doesn't beat manual forecasts or adoption stays low; otherwise scale across the region, then offer it as a global standard.

  8. Change

    Deal specialists help design it; approval committees agree the memo format; monthly value report.

Data and dashboard governance: one truth for data and for meaning

In Horizons 1–2, teams use AI tools to build their own dashboards. The data has one source, but the meaning drifts as each view defines and filters KPIs differently. Govern the metrics and the decision forums, not the building.

Metrics

Certify the metrics, not just the data

Each core KPI (AANR, time to revenue, realisation, NRR, coverage, forecast accuracy) defined once, with an owner and a formula, in a shared metric layer. Every dashboard and AI tool reads from it, so a copilot’s answer is the certified number.

Tiers

Three tiers with a promotion path

Certified, shared and personal. Self-built dashboards that many people use, or that a forum asks for, are promoted to certified. Builders become a source of ideas, not a governance problem.

Forums

Enforce through decision forums

The forecast call, QBR and deal council accept only certified numbers. Backed by quarterly usage-based clean-up, duplicate detection and a small metrics council that approves definition changes.

One $100 card payment

Compensation and quota mechanics

What's documented
  • Sellers on a Sales Incentive Plan: ~62/38 base to variable, accelerators above 100%, paid on AANR + go-live + MBOs
  • MIP/TIP team and market overlays; VAS-aligned incentives
  • Ops staff on the corporate bonus (VIP, 8–25% of base) + RSUs; sales engineers on a non-quota bonus
  • ~60% of sellers reach quota (self-reported)
How multi-year deals count inferred
  • AANR = one year of the deal model's net revenue, credited once at signing (like ACV, but net of incentives and based on forecast volume)
  • From year 2 the deal is part of the existing base; quota comes from new products, portfolios and conversions on top
  • Renewals usually lower net revenue, so they're best measured by a retention objective
  • Annual quota = plan target minus revenue already contracted, split by account potential
