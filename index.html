The sales operations engine

Four stages run as a yearly loop: Design where and how we sell, Plan how much and by whom, Govern whether we're on track and making good deals, and Improve so every cycle runs better than the last. Automate runs underneath all four: each step is redesigned around data, tools and AI.

Key steps by stage

01DesignWhere and how do we sell?
Key steps
  1. Go-to-market model and sales motions: direct, specialist overlay, partner, digital
  2. Segmentation logic: client value × potential × needs
  3. Roles and role charters: client directors, specialists, engineers, digital, partners
  4. Coverage model and account density per tier
  5. Rules of engagement: who sells what to whom; crediting and split rules
  6. Comp plan design: measures, pay mix, accelerators, quota policy
  7. Deal policy: pricing guardrails, incentive and VIK benchmarks, approval tiers
Outputs

Go-to-market blueprint · segment and coverage map · role charters · crediting rules · comp plans · approval matrix

KPIs

Coverage cost as % of revenue · accounts per seller · share of whitespace covered

02PlanHow much, by whom, with what?
Key steps
  1. Multi-year plan and annual operating plan
  2. Budget split by cluster, market, segment and product
  3. Capacity planning: productivity per role, ramp, attrition, hiring plan
  4. Territory and account assignment
  5. Quota setting and allocation, with buffers (over-assignment, holdbacks)
  6. Account planning: whitespace, relationship map, renewal calendar
  7. Demand planning: pipeline targets by source, sales plays, marketing campaigns

Annual plan · quota letters · territory files · capacity and hiring plan · account plans · play and campaign calendar

KPIs

  2. Forecasting and pipeline governance: categories, hygiene, accuracy
  3. Deal governance: deal desk, approvals, contracting
  4. VIK management: budgets, drawdown, utilisation
  5. Performance management: scorecards, early warnings, interventions
  6. Comp administration: crediting, commission runs, disputes, exceptions
  7. Post-deal tracking: realisation vs deal model, Signed-Not-Live

KPIs

02 · VISION

Best-in-class sales operations for Visa CEMEA

Visa's scarce resource isn't only seller time. It's the incentive budget: $15.8B a year paid back to clients. Sales ops exists to put each of those dollars where it buys the most durable volume.

A commercial control tower that maximises net revenue per incentive dollar and shortens the time from signature to revenue, run as one connected system from plan to realised revenue, with AI doing the routine work.

01Commercial strategy & planning
Best in class
  • Segments by client value and potential; coverage tiers: dedicated, pooled, digital, partner
  • Territories balanced on potential; capacity model linked to the annual and multi-year plan
  • Plan gap = target minus revenue already contracted; account plans for every key client
Priorities for CEMEA
  • Cluster playbooks: the GCC (cross-border, domestic schemes), Africa (cash conversion, fintechs), Central Europe and Central Asia (mature renewals)
  • Coverage planned around the renewal calendar; scaled digital coverage for the long tail of 1,800+ clients
  • Defend domestic share against Jaywan, mada and instant A2A payments

KPIs: whitespace per seller · coverage cost · Visa share of client volume

02Performance management, quota & incentives
Best in class
  • One scorecard from region to seller; a fixed monthly, quarterly and annual review rhythm
  • Quotas set on account potential and issued within 30–45 days of the year starting; no more than three plan measures
  • Pay on net, realised revenue; retention measured separately from growth
Priorities for CEMEA
  • Co-design incentive plans with Global Sales Operations, Finance and HR; own quota setting and allocation
  • Partial true-up of seller pay to realised revenue; a retention objective with an incentive ceiling for renewals
  • Forecast accuracy tracked by market every month

KPIs: 60–70% of sellers at quota · forecast accuracy · AANR vs plan

03Sales process, deal governance & lead-to-cash
Best in class
  • One methodology built into CRM stages with exit criteria; one definition of "qualified"
  • Tiered approvals with published turnaround targets; one deal model; standard contract templates
  • Lead-to-cash run end to end with Product, Finance, Legal, Technology and Operations
Priorities for CEMEA
  • Governance as a service: approval turnaround by tier, actual deal results fed back into benchmarks
  • Contracting and VIK: track utilisation and drawdown, tie VIK to client activation, flag unused budgets well before renewal
  • Signed-Not-Live managed as a pipeline stage with named owners and deadlines

KPIs: approval turnaround · incentive-to-gross vs benchmark · VIK utilisation · time to revenue · realisation

04Enablement & capability
Best in class
  • Role-based onboarding and accreditation; manager coaching based on deal data
  • Methodology (IDDEA) built into stages and deal reviews, not just training
  • Sales plays per product; one governed source of content, with stale material retired
Priorities for CEMEA
  • Adapt global enablement programmes to each cluster's needs
  • Regional sales plays for value-added services, commercial payments and Visa Direct
  • Build commercial acumen in payments economics: deal models, incentives, realisation

KPIs: time to productivity · pipeline from sales plays · content use vs win rate

05Sales technology, data & AI
Best in class
  • CRM as the single source of truth, with named data owners and standard metric definitions
  • CRM, CPQ, deal, contract and billing data connected
  • A multi-year AI roadmap and a culture of AI fluency, with adoption targets per leader
Priorities for CEMEA
  • Join Visa's transaction data with CRM and deal data, an edge no software company has
  • Year 1: admin agents and a deal-desk copilot pilot. Year 2: renewal-risk warning and VAS whitespace. Year 3: predictive forecasting and planning
  • Data readiness first: fix data quality before scaling models

KPIs: data completeness · seller selling time · tool adoption · forecast error

06Leadership & stakeholder management
Best in class
  • Trusted advisor to regional leadership on growth, resources and transformation
  • Clear decision rights: global sets the standard, the region adapts, markets execute
  • A diverse, high-performing team with clear career paths
Priorities for CEMEA
  • Be the two-way bridge between CEMEA leadership and Global Sales Operations
  • Data-driven recommendations for investment and resource allocation
  • One operating rhythm across 80+ markets, with room for local relevance

KPIs: initiatives delivered vs plan · team engagement · regretted attrition

03 · OUTSIDE-IN VIEW

My understanding of Visa's commercial engine

Built from public filings, Investor Day 2025, earnings calls and job postings. Items marked inferred are industry practice or my reading, not published by Visa.

Strategy: a network of networks

Investor Day 2025

Be the best way to pay and be paid, across every flow

  • Consumer payments: ~$41T of consumer spend, ~$23T still on cash, legacy rails or domestic schemes
  • Commercial & money movement: ~$200T of B2B flows and ~$145T of other money movement; ~$115T actively pursued
  • Value-added services: a $520B opportunity, ~2% captured
  • Foundation: Visa-as-a-Service, an unbundled platform sold capability by capability
Where it stands (Q3 FY26)

Growth is shifting to services and new flows

  • Net revenue $11.6B, +14%; full-year outlook raised
  • VAS $3.8B, +34%, about a third of revenue
  • CMS revenue +17%; Visa Direct transactions +21%
  • New bets: agentic commerce, stablecoins, issuer processing (Pismo + DPS)
  • ~7% of roles cut in July 2026, with savings reinvested in growth

Illustrative

One $100 card payment

The merchant pays roughly $2 in fees. The issuing bank takes the largest share as interchange (Visa sets it but doesn't keep it). The acquirer keeps its margin. Visa earns a few cents of network fees, and several times more if the card is used abroad.

Visa takes no credit risk. Incentives are 28% of gross revenue and are growing faster than net revenue, which is why deal governance matters so much.

Sales coverage and organisation

Global

GSCO

Global Sales & Commercial Operations, under the SVP Sales Transformation, sets the operating model, scorecards, incentive design, tools and deal governance, and runs Sales 2.0.

Regional

BPO / Sales Excellence

Each region's sales ops head advises the Regional President and owns business reviews, quota setting, VIK budgets and AI strategy.

Product

BU commercial ops

VAS, CMS and Visa Direct each run their own commercial operations for specialist sales motions.

  • ~4,000 sellers globally (Highspot case study). ~450 VAS specialists and ~5,400 resale partners; CMS specialist sellers grew ~3x from FY21 to FY24.
  • Coverage tiers: dedicated teams for Tier 1 banks, pooled coverage for Tier 2, digital and inside sales for Tier 3 and fintechs. One client team owns each bank through the full contract term.
  • Sales 2.0: the programme to become "a best-in-class B2B sales organization", covering segmentation and coverage, methodology, performance management, incentives and deal economics, and tools and AI.
  • IDDEA: Visa's consultative selling methodology (now IDDEA 2.0 in CEMEA), delivered through bootcamps, quarterly accreditation and AI-scored pitch practice in Highspot.

Compensation and quota mechanics

What's documented
  • Sellers on a Sales Incentive Plan: ~62/38 base to variable, accelerators above 100%, paid on AANR + go-live + MBOs
  • MIP/TIP team and market overlays; VAS-aligned incentives
  • Ops staff on the corporate bonus (VIP, 8–25% of base) + RSUs; sales engineers on a non-quota bonus
  • ~60% of sellers reach quota (self-reported)
How multi-year deals count inferred
  • AANR = one year of the deal model's net revenue, credited once at signing (like ACV, but net of incentives and based on forecast volume)
  • From year 2 the deal is part of the existing base; quota comes from new products, portfolios and conversions on top
  • Renewals usually lower net revenue, so they're best measured by a retention objective
  • Annual quota = plan target minus revenue already contracted, split by account potential

CEMEA context

Competing rails

Domestic schemes (Jaywan, mada) and instant A2A payments (Aani, sarie) compete for domestic volume.

Cross-border hub

Tourism, expatriate spending and remittances make the GCC rich in Visa's most profitable flow.

A region in motion

A new UAE–Kuwait–Qatar region from January 2026; cash conversion across Africa; a live testbed for agentic payments and dirham stablecoins.

Industry trends to watch

New rails

Stablecoins

~$300B+ in circulation; the US GENIUS Act is enforceable from January 2027. Visa settles in stablecoins at a ~$20B annualised run rate and is "multi-coin, multi-chain."

New buyers

Agentic commerce

AI agents paying within user-set limits, using agent tokens and "know your agent" checks. Early, with standards unsettled; B2B is likely to adopt it first.

New products

Tokens, B2B, fraud

Tokenisation and passkeys, digitising B2B payments, and AI-driven scams make value-added services the margin story.
